Privacy Policy for Chainhop

Last updated: 4 October 2026

This policy explains how the Android game Chainhop (package com.chainhop.game) handles information. It applies to the app only.

Short version

Chainhop has no accounts, no sign-in, and no analytics, and the developer receives no personal information about you. The game itself stores your progress only on your own device.

Chainhop does show ads, and it does sell optional in-app purchases. Both are operated by Google, not by the developer. Google's advertising SDK collects data about your device — including your advertising ID — in order to show and measure ads. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland you are asked to consent before that happens, and you can change your answer at any time. The sections below set out exactly what is collected and by whom.

Advertising, and your choices

Chainhop shows two kinds of ad, both only in the Chain Rush mode:

  • A full-screen ad after some Chain Rush runs end. Buying Remove Ads or the Premium Bundle turns these off permanently.
  • Rewarded videos that you start yourself by tapping a button, in exchange for extra hops, a life, or a cosmetic item. These are always optional and are never shown unless you ask for one. They remain available after you buy Remove Ads, because the purchase removes interruptions rather than rewards.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the app asks for your consent through Google's User Messaging Platform before it requests any ad. If consent is required and you have not given it, Chainhop requests no ads at all and simply runs without them. You can change your choice at any time from inside the game: Shop → Passes → Ad privacy → Options.

Chainhop does not use any third-party ad network other than Google AdMob. No mediation partners are enabled.

In-app purchases

Chainhop sells three one-time, non-consumable items: Premium Bundle, Unlimited Lives and Remove Ads. There is no subscription.

Payment is taken by Google Play. The developer never sees or stores your card details, billing address, or name. The app asks Google Play which of these items your Google account owns — when it starts, and when you make a purchase — and records only a yes/no flag per item in its local save file. If a purchase is refunded or cancelled, that check removes the item at the next launch.

Information stored on your device

So the game can remember where you were, Chainhop saves the following in its own private storage area on your device:

  • Level progress, star ratings, and best scores.
  • Your selected colour theme and peg style, and which ones you have unlocked.
  • Your Chain Rush best runs, kept only on this device.
  • Your current life count and the time the last one was used, so lives refill correctly.
  • Whether you own each in-app purchase.

This save data never leaves your device, is not readable by other apps, and Android deletes it when you uninstall the game. You can clear it at any time through Android's Settings → Apps → Chainhop → Storage → Clear data. Clearing it does not forfeit a purchase: ownership lives with your Google account and is restored the next time the game starts.

Google's SDKs keep their own data on the device and on Google's systems; that is covered by the Google policies linked above, and deleting the app's data does not reset your advertising ID. You can reset that yourself in Android settings.

Permissions

Chainhop requests VIBRATE for haptic feedback when pegs move, and INTERNET together with ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE because ads and purchases need a network connection. It also declares com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID, which is what lets the advertising SDK read the advertising ID.

A few further permissions appear in the installed app because Google's advertising and billing libraries declare them for their own background work — among them WAKE_LOCK, FOREGROUND_SERVICE and BIND_JOB_SERVICE. Chainhop's own code does not use them. The game asks for no access to your location, contacts, photos, files, microphone, or camera.

Children

Chainhop is not directed to children. Its Google Play target audience is 13 and over, and it is not enrolled in the Designed for Families programme. The app does not signal to Google that its users are under the age of consent, so it should not be used by children under 13. The developer does not knowingly collect any information from children; if you believe a child has used the app and you want its advertising identifier reset, you can do that in Android settings, or write to the address below.

Your rights

Because the developer holds no personal data about you, there is nothing on the developer's side to access, correct, export, or erase. Requests concerning the data Google holds should be made to Google using the links above; in the game you can withdraw ad consent at any time through Shop → Passes → Ad privacy, and you can remove all locally stored data by uninstalling the app or clearing its storage.

Changes to this policy

This policy is updated before any release that changes what the app collects, and the date at the top changes with it. The 2 September 2026 version described a build with no ads, no purchases and no internet access; this version covers the first release that has all three. Continuing to use the app after an update means the revised policy applies.
